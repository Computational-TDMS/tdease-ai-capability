# Object and mass interpretation

Use this reference when a source frame or object identity is ambiguous.

A PrSM is a spectrum interpretation; a proteoform is a molecular form; a feature
is an observed mass/charge/RT signal. Multiple PrSMs can describe one feature,
and co-isolation can mix different precursors. Preserve these relations rather
than merging objects from similar masses or labels.

Reference-sequence differences may combine terminal processing, substitutions,
isoforms, covalent modifications and noncovalent adducts. Select alternatives
relevant to the observations; a library mass match does not account for all
these mechanisms.

Distinguish reported monoisotopic mass, the observed isotope-envelope apex and
average mass. The monoisotopic peak may be too weak to observe. Near-integer
isotope-spacing residuals invite a registration check; their spacing alone
does not prove an artifact. Compare neutral Da, m/z and ppm in their stated
frames.

Repeated MS2 selections can add fragment coverage, but they are not independent
biological replicates. Source-linked precursor, isolation and activation facts
determine whether pooling is meaningful. Non-detection depends on acquisition
and sensitivity.

Quantification belongs to a defined estimator and experimental design. Charge
aggregation, normalization and missing-value treatment come from that method,
not from a universal label-free recipe.
