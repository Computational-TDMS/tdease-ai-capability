# Interpreting spectral support

| Claim | Relevant observations | Remaining ambiguity |
|---|---|---|
| Mass/composition compatibility | Feature envelope, charge and mass with QC | Registration, calibration, interference and competing compositions |
| Backbone support | Activation-appropriate fragment matches and continuity | Shared sequences and unexplained signal |
| Chemical model discrimination | Observed predictions differing between complete alternatives | Alternatives not tested and acquisition sensitivity |
| Site localization | Observed fragments that separate the proposed placements | Placements with identical observed predictions |

These are claim distinctions, not a required assay sequence. Report equivalent
placements as a span or set; a likely site is not thereby a measured site.

## Alternative explanations

- Isotope assignment can produce near-integer mass residuals; spacing alone
  does not identify their cause.
- Adducts and sample-handling chemistry can recur across a dataset. Biological
  and preparation origins require context; the chemical name alone does not
  establish origin.
- Near-isobaric models require sufficient measured resolving power and mass
  accuracy in the relevant region.
- Broad tolerances, many ion series and charge states increase accidental
  matches. Interpret match counts with the error model and discriminating ions.
- Unmatched signal may reflect an incomplete model, co-isolation or unmodeled
  fragmentation. Its intensity alone does not invalidate the supported matches.

State what was observed, which alternatives it separates, and the assumptions
or sensitivity limits. A predicted ion not observed in a defined search is
negative evidence only to the extent that the acquisition could detect it.

An unresolved comparison is a valid result. Continue when another available
observation could materially change the conclusion; otherwise report the
evidence limit and the useful next discriminator.
